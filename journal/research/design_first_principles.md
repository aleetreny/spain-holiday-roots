# Do Spanish holidays retrace the rural exodus?

Research design for *EPJ Data Science* · 27 November 2026

## Recommendation

Lead with **whether trips from Spanish cities reach the specific small municipalities where city-born residents' parents were born, and when that alignment strengthens**. Use the municipal INE series for geography and recurrence, the annual census for the historical cohort contrast, and MITMA for named holiday nights. The case for *EPJ Data Science* rests on combining independently produced aggregate traces and survey data, testing what survives the publication threshold, and making the comparison reproducible. [Journal aims and scope](https://link.springer.com/journal/13688/aims-and-scope), accessed 27 November 2026.

The clearest pilot result is on the extensive margin. Among 789,254 eligible interprovincial pairs, having a surveyed parental link is associated with a **3.90 percentage point** larger August–February increase in the probability that a flow appears in the INE release (95% CI 3.04–4.75 pp, 2022–2026). The August–October difference is **3.15 pp** (2.42–3.89, 2022–2025). Among pairs published in all ten February/August months, the corresponding count-growth difference is **0.056 log points** (−0.027 to 0.140). A consistent summer alignment across flows of at least 30 trips is a defensible prospect for the paper. The evidence for greater counts among consistently observed pairs is less precise and depends on the baseline and support. Neither margin identifies who travelled, the traveller's motive, or how many extra overnights would disappear if family ties were absent. [Municipal calendar output](../pilots/design/out/municipal_calendar.csv).

## What to estimate

Let $i$ be the current city of residence, $j$ a mainland municipality of up to 2,000 residents in 2021, $p(j)$ its province, and $t$ a month or a night. The municipal target population is interprovincial pairs from the disclosed ECEPOV cities. The surveys and phone tables are linked by geography only. Define $R^1_{ij}$ as the survey-weighted share of sampled city residents born in $j$, and $R^2_{ij}$ as the weighted share of city-born residents' parent slots with a birth in $j$, with each of the two slots contributing half the person's weight. For a presence specification use at least one distinct reporting household, and retain the count of distinct households for reliability. Report missing parent information, exposed dyads and denominator separately by origin. Treat an unobserved survey tie as a sampling zero, with uncertainty from household resampling and a sensitivity that requires two independent sampled households. [ECEPOV 2021 and documentation](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/INEbase/es/operacion.htm?c=Estadistica_C&cid=1254736177092&menu=resultados), accessed 27 November 2026.

1. **Exact-location premium.** In a month or a named night, estimate the difference in expected flows across comparably located villages in the same province from the same city, for a contrast in $R^2$, controlling jointly for $R^1$. Use city × destination-province and village fixed effects, and flexible road travel time. The effects compare a city's allocation within a province and adjust for each village's general attraction. With two positive exposures, in a log-link model with a genuine $\log R^2$ regressor, an elasticity of $\beta$ gives a flow ratio $2^\beta$ for doubling the exposure. A presence model or $\log(1+R^2)$ count specification has a different interpretation. Report prevalence and marginal effects on an interpretable scale. This level estimand still includes stable bilateral preferences and predicts geographic alignment.

2. **Holiday premium.** Use a panel with dyad effects $\alpha_{ij}$, city × destination-province × period effects $\gamma_{i,p(j),t}$, and village × period effects $\delta_{jt}$. Estimate interactions of predetermined parental and first-generation ties with the months or named nights, plus calendar-specific road-time and income × amenity interactions where there is support. Under a log link, $\beta_H$ on $R^2_{ij}\times H_t$ is a difference in log flow ratios relative to the reference period. With the release indicator as outcome, report the analogous change in publication probability. Use a balanced geographic frame chosen before looking at holiday outcomes. For INE, estimate publication and published-count margins separately. Pairwise log differences on common published support are a transparent check. Use PPML for the full count outcome only where a release-independent observed count, or a defensible treatment of unpublished cells, is available. The minimum observed value of 30 is not enough to certify a deterministic censoring rule on scaled counts. If the exact rule is documented, show interval-based sensitivity and a censored likelihood with dispersion checks. If it remains ambiguous, aggregate to more completely observed units for the count validation. Keep effect sizes for February and October, identify Easter by its actual March/April dates, and use a rolling calendar of all nonholiday dates before settling a daily comparison.

3. **Historical specificity and change.** In the Censo Anual data compare destination provinces' ties from pre-1981 arrivals in the current province against more recent arrivals, jointly with birthplace size and age structure. Report within-age comparisons and recent-migrant negative controls, and interact the municipal parental estimate with historical depopulation and resident age bands where the ECEPOV support allows it. Arrival year to the current province is an imperfect proxy for exodus timing and the municipal parent histories do not date the moves. A trend in a 2021 exposure's August premium from 2019 to 2026 measures the persistence of an association with a fixed exposure. Demographic cohort fading would require repeated measurements of comparable cohorts, evidence about their changing population shares, and explicit assumptions about age, period and cohort. Use pre-pandemic months for context, flag disruption in 2020–2021, and treat the 2022–2026 straight-line slope as descriptive. [Censo Anual microdata](https://www.ine.es/ftp/microdatos/censoanual/vc/datos_muestra_2024.zip), accessed 27 November 2026.

4. **How much extra travel is associated with roots?** As an optional descriptive calculation, for each city × destination province, hold the holiday margin fixed and predict the allocation with the fitted holiday interaction. Recalculate the allocation with that interaction set to zero, preserving the ordinary-day roots term and re-normalising both sets of fitted flows to the same holiday margin. Sum the positive changes across parental-linked destinations to estimate the **model-implied redistribution towards those villages**, and show negative changes elsewhere. Use total positive baseline-to-holiday flow gains over the same area and support as denominator, with the baseline scaled for month length or averaged over comparable nights. Publish the quantities alongside the ratio, bootstrap, and assess denominator and suppression sensitivities. Because the total is held fixed by construction, this answers where additional flows went. Only attempt it if the count model can be validated against aggregates and the support and unit are clear. Municipal INE yields trips, so a person-night version requires MITMA zones or published province night totals and its own estimand.

### Main competing explanations

|---|---|---|
| Beach, weather, ski and other amenities | Compare different cities travelling to the same village on the same date and villages in the same province from the same city. Re-estimate for municipalities in entirely inland provinces and, with real municipal geometry, inland villages within coastal provinces. Test Christmas Eve, All Saints and Good Friday against weekday-matched weeks and the rest of the calendar. Use within-province matched alternative villages as a falsification of exact-place specificity. | Cities can differ in destination-specific tastes, and winter events also attract non-family visitors. |
| Distance, roads and travel cost | Use dyad effects for stable accessibility, with travel-time bins × calendar and short- versus long-haul interactions. Compare nearby villages with similar routes and road times. | Second homes held by unrelated owners | Use village-by-period effects to absorb shared availability, revisit the estimates in villages with low non-primary stock, and check owner-household region matches in ECEPOV. ## Pilots

### 1. Municipal calendar and strict second-generation ties

I matched people born in their exact current city to small municipalities named as a parent's birthplace. The primary regressor is a surveyed parental tie, with a separate first-generation presence term. The static frame is 137 cities × 5,849 villages off the home province (789,254 dyads). Only 7,988 pairs have a sampled strict parental tie and 1,389 have two or more reporting households. I estimated differences with destination and city × destination-province effects, plus log distance and its square, equivalent to using dyad effects and period interactions in a two-period linear model. Geometry is represented by the primary MITMA zone. Intervals use a destination-province clustered $t$ approximation conditional on the survey ties. They do not incorporate ECEPOV sampling uncertainty or test all calendar months jointly. Calendar rows compare each available month to February of its year where available, with October as an additional August baseline and the reference for all available 2019 months. [Script](../pilots/design/municipal_calendar.py), [sample](../pilots/design/out/municipal_sample.csv), [estimates](../pilots/design/out/municipal_calendar.csv).

| Aug − Feb, 2022–2026 mean | +3.90 pp [3.04, 4.75] | +0.056 [−0.027, 0.140], 2,372 dyads |
| Aug − Oct, 2022–2025 mean | +3.15 pp [2.42, 3.89] | +0.050 [0.015, 0.084], 3,611 dyads |

The November and December rows are whole-month comparisons. All five 2022–2026 August–February release coefficients fall between 3.44 and 4.26 pp. Raising the release-count threshold to 60 and 100 trips gives +3.28 [2.52, 4.05] and +2.85 [2.04, 3.66] pp. Among continuously published pairs above those thresholds, the point estimate moves from +0.096 to −0.043 log points and both intervals include zero. A second reporting household is associated with a further +4.55 pp [1.99, 7.11] over a one-household tie, which also reflects the strength and detectability of larger diaspora links. The trend interval leaves room for modest changes, and the design cannot distinguish cohort replacement. [Threshold and survey-support checks](../pilots/design/out/municipal_robustness.csv).

### 2. Held-out August predictions

I trained the same shallow boosted-tree classifiers and Poisson-loss regressors on August 2022–2024 and evaluated on August 2025 and 2026. The static features are size, distance, coast proximity, origin income and historical loss. The stronger baseline adds current February, previous August and previous October released dyad counts. Adding roots provides weighted birthplace counts for both generations and parental household support. Counts missing from the historic release remain missing. The held-out outcomes and features apply to the same geography, so this tests future periods with known cities and villages. Province bootstrap intervals describe test-set variation conditional on the fitted models. The comparisons use the observed release process, with count loss evaluated only for published positive flows. A retrospective time split freezes the 2024 origins' income and population for all training years. [Script](../pilots/design/august_prediction.py), [full scores](../pilots/design/out/august_prediction.csv).

| Test August | Baseline | Reduction in publication log loss | Reduction in published-count Poisson deviance |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 2025 | Geography | 2.12% [1.43, 2.81] | 4.18% [1.76, 6.40] |
| 2026 | Geography | 1.80% [1.15, 2.45] | 3.55% [1.32, 5.80] |
| 2025 | Geography + lagged flows | 0.57% [0.33, 0.81] | 0.23% [−0.22, 0.76] |

For publication, average precision moves from 0.7163 to 0.7188 in 2025 and from 0.7943 to 0.7954 in 2026 with lagged flows. The defensible prediction result is a small improvement in predicting the released network given historical flows and a larger improvement with geography alone. A final predictive check should include calibrated gravity and radiation baselines, same-month last year and lagged-flow tests, and geographical holdouts. Report uncertainty in paired losses and keep the count outcome conditional on the criterion used to define it. [Radiation model](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10856), accessed 27 November 2026.

### 3. Local holidays feasibility

I reconciled Castilla y León's export by code and normalised municipality name to avoid assigning a hamlet's holiday to its parent municipality. I required a direct, one-municipality MITMA zone and compared the two nights around the event with the same two weekdays at ±7 days. I excluded windows close to another recognised local date, regional and national holidays or the October 2024 disruption. The full daily index supports **32 events in 18 villages** of up to 5,000 residents (seven provinces and 30 events in villages with a parental tie).
