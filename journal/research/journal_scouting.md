### 2.4 Synthesis: What a Typical Accepted Paper Looks Like in Each Journal

- ***EPJ Data Science***: Long-form computational social-science papers (**~6,900–12,000 body words**, **45–75 references**, **3–9 multi-panel figures**, **0–4 tables**, plus rich Supplementary Information). Reviewers expect formal **null-model / baseline benchmarking** (gravity, radiation, rewiring), explicit validation of digital-trace biases (e.g. home-detection error, operator market share), open code/data statements, and transparent limitations.
- ***Journal of Transport Geography***: Tighter, map-and-model empirical transport-geography papers (**~5,300–6,500 body words**, 8–12 typeset pages, **50–65 references**, **2–5 figures** [almost always including choropleth/flow maps], **3–5 tables**). Reviewers prioritise **spatial/geographical interpretation**, transport-system and territorial-planning implications (distance decay, seasonal capacity/corridors, accessibility), and validation of mobile-phone OD matrices against official census/survey data, rather than standalone graph-topology indices.
- ***Population, Space and Place***: Substantively driven population-geography papers (**~6,100–9,700 body words**, **70–90 references**, **0–5 figures**, **0–3 tables**). Strongly structured around a dedicated **Theoretical / Demographic Background** section (`Introduction → Theoretical background → Data and methods → Results → Discussion and conclusion`). Reviewers expect deep engagement with migration, life-course/generational ties, rural depopulation, VFR, and second-home literatures, with methods explained cleanly for quantitative geographers and demographers rather than statistical physicists.
