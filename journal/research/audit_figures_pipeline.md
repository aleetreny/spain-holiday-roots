## Editorial judgement

The strongest current result is that **birthplace ties predict where cities send overnight visitors, and ties to earlier arrivals and village-born residents become more predictive in inland summer flows**. The pooled summer increase depends on the comparison period. The strongest potential journal contribution is a cross-scale test of whether a birthplace network predicts *which* municipalities a city's visitors go to within a destination province, beyond geography, destination demand and the ordinary strength of the corridor. Make that test survive disclosure thresholds, noisy roots counts and a held-out period before putting it in the title. The aggregate question is worth asking even if the municipal association fails. A title asking whether individuals return to their family's own village promises a linkage these data cannot make.

The village switch is a complementary result about the receiving end of the mobility network. A one-panel analogue of the present topology result should establish the role of network density and the degree sequence, then give way to the roots and village results. EPJ Data Science's stated scope accommodates a statistically rigorous study of this kind and does not require a collection of network metrics. Its Regular Article instructions require an availability statement and information on the minimum data needed to interpret and replicate the findings. Clean-roots extracts, date and geography manifests, code and a demonstration input will matter more than the number of null models. [Journal scope](https://link.springer.com/journal/13688/aims-and-scope) and [Regular Article instructions](https://link.springer.com/journal/13688/submission-guidelines/regular-article), accessed 27 November 2026.
