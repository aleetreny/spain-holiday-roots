# EPJ Data Science house style for the Spain holiday mobility paper

**Source access date throughout: 27 November 2026.** Counts below are my counts. This is a purposive sample of published articles close to our question. It describes editorial precedent. The sample cannot estimate acceptance probabilities.

## The strongest fit

Lead with an empirical result about **how past internal migration is associated with present-day holiday flows**. The existing city-to-province result is heterogeneous by arrival cohort, birthplace size and destination type. The pooled increase varies with the choice of ordinary dates. The most promising extension is a municipality-scale, multi-year test of the same association, with survey-based parental origins as a distinct source of evidence if the relevant cells support inference. The existing overnight network gives the flow counts, national coverage and a spatial baseline. Keep rewiring in the main paper where it clarifies what the aggregate network can explain. [Coursework results](../../reports/manuscript/sections/04_results.tex), [coursework discussion](../../reports/manuscript/sections/05_discussion.tex), [journal scope][scope].

The closest holiday precedent is He et al., which compares two Spring Festivals in Nanjing, including the homecoming phase. Its contribution is post-reopening mobility and an activity-sequence method. It makes a holiday-plus-network framing alone insufficient as a novelty claim. Tafani et al. make migrant heterogeneity and the sampling frame central. Lucchini et al. combine location traces with a census measure at a different level of observation. Den Nijs et al. test how well a measurement model travels across places. The accepted abstract of Kang and Hong puts gravity, day-off conditions and age within the same design. Our empirical distinction is the historical connection between origin cities and destination provinces or municipalities, evaluated across holiday and ordinary periods. [He][he], [Tafani][taf], [Lucchini][luc], [den Nijs][den], [Kang and Hong](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjds/s13688-026-00702-w).

## 3. Figure style and references

**Figure 1 has a job.** Five papers open with a workflow or model schematic, counting Kwon's schematic-plus-example as a workflow. Two start with study geography or sample coverage. One opens with a compact findings overview and two with analytical results. These are my classifications from the ten linked figures. There is precedent for a design diagram, a map with analytical context, and an immediate result. A calendar alone leaves our key inferential step unexplained. [He, Fig. 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjds/s13688-026-00622-9/figures/1), [Tafani, Fig. 1][taf1], [Mendoza-Hurtado, Fig. 1][men1], [Bouzaghrane][bou], [Aydoğdu][ayd], [Lucchini][luc], [Tang, Fig. 1][tang1], [Kwon][kw], [Andris][and], [den Nijs, Fig. 1][den1].

**Panels, maps and uncertainty.** Lowercase panel labels are common, with punctuation varying. Andris uses uppercase letters. The clearest comparisons repeat scales across panels, reserve strong colour for data or the comparator and keep the basemap quiet. Tafani places coverage and a distribution beside a map. Den Nijs combines unadorned line/scatter panels with a single sequential map scale. Mendoza-Hurtado uses a dotted path for a different validation operation. Lucchini uses time-series ribbons for standard errors and Tafani uses coefficient plots with uncertainty intervals. The caption tells readers what the marks and bands represent. For our design, use consistent lower-case panel letters and axes with units, one scale for paired maps, colour plus shape or line style, and separate confidence intervals, null envelopes and spread across overlapping nights. [Tafani][taf], [den Nijs][den], [Mendoza-Hurtado][men], [Lucchini][luc], [Andris][and].

**Caption and size.** Median caption length is 44 words across 62 main figures, ranging from 2 to 212 (my counts, same token convention; appendix figures excluded). A schematic can use a short sentence. A multi-panel inference figure needs one compact panel clause or sentence plus the denominator, baseline and interval definition. Target 35–75 words initially and allow longer explanatory legends where necessary. Sample panels use compact sans-serif labels, with bolder panel letters. Figure fonts vary across articles. **Abstract template.** Use one paragraph. Start with the mobility phenomenon and the unresolved comparison in two sentences. Name both input data sources, their time coverage and the observational unit. Name the reference periods and the spatial model in one sentence. Give the pooled estimate under two baselines and the main subgroup and inland contrasts with a clearly specified unit. End by saying what aggregate holiday flows can reveal about the persistence of earlier internal migration.

**Introduction closing template.** State the dyadic quantity and why a simple seasonal map cannot identify it. Name the census measure and the mobility source, then state the comparison and what it holds fixed. In a second sentence report the baseline dependence and specify the cohort/destination result that changes the interpretation. A third sentence can give the strongest independent check and its limits. Keep the roadmap to one sentence if the paper needs it. A usable draft closing follows.

> I test whether places connected by earlier internal migration also exchange more overnight visitors during holidays. I link census counts of residents by province of birth to mobile-phone estimates of overnight stays, compare cities within the same origin province and account for distance, income and ordinary-night flows. The overall August change depends on the reference dates. The clearest increases are on routes associated with pre-1981 arrivals and village-born residents, especially for inland destinations. I check how these results vary with the choice of nights, geographic units and treatment of empty census cells. The comparison measures ties between places and does not identify who travels.

Use the last sentence about checks only for checks in the final paper. The coursework measures a city–province association. A successful municipal or parental-origins extension could replace one of the checks with a new result. ## 6. Recommended version of our paper

### Title options

1. **Historical internal migration and holiday mobility in Spain** (8 words). The strongest default. It names the two phenomena and works at provincial or municipal resolution.
2. **The imprint of internal migration on Spain’s holiday mobility** (9 words). More memorable. Use if the historical contrast holds across dates and years.
3. **Do holiday flows retrace internal migration? Evidence from mainland Spain** (10 words). A question whose unit is flows. The abstract should immediately qualify the answer by cohort, destination and baseline.
4. **Birthplace ties and inland holiday travel in mainland Spain** (9 words). The most precise option for the coursework-based paper.
5. **A seasonal geography of migration ties: Evidence from Spain’s overnight mobility** (11 words). Emphasises time and scale. Use with a broader multi-year seasonal design.

Title word lengths above use the same token convention as the sample. Skip the double rhetorical hook and claims about families returning to specific villages unless the requisite individual evidence is available.

### Abstract skeleton

*225 words by my count. Square brackets show values to carry over only if they remain the final estimates. References and move labels are omitted in the submission version.*

> Holiday travel changes where people spend the night, but it is difficult to distinguish the destinations associated with past migration from those favoured because they are nearby or on the coast. I test whether the geography of internal migration is associated with overnight holiday travel in mainland Spain. I link mobile-phone estimates of stays across [2,435 zones] in 2024 to census counts of city residents by province of birth. I estimate how flows vary with birthplace ties, comparing cities from the same origin province at the same destinations and accounting for distance and income. The average increase in the birthplace-tie elasticity in August depended on the comparison dates: [+0.096] against February and [+0.137] against October weekdays. The increases associated with residents who arrived in their current province before 1981 and those born in villages were clearer against February, at [+0.084] and [+0.086]. Both contrasts were larger for inland stays, at [+0.154] and [+0.123]. In the fifth of zones with the greatest past population losses, the share receiving more people than they sent rose from [24%] in February to [99%] in August. The census groups describe residents and the flow data count overnight stays for all residents of the origin city. These patterns show how earlier internal migration can remain visible in the seasonal redistribution of people across places.

The revised abstract should replace a secondary statistic with a held-out-year or municipality-level estimate if that test adds credible independent evidence. If reporting the pooled contrast, show its uncertainty in the main paper and keep both reference periods visible. ### Style rules for drafting

1. Put the observational claim and its reference period in the title, abstract and introduction. Use the same definitions of flow, birthplace tie and historical cohort throughout.
2. Use concrete, active British English. First-person singular is appropriate for a sole author and first-person plural for co-authors. Use present tense for the question and figures, and past tense for analysed records and completed procedures.
3. Give the decisive baseline and interval beside each central estimate. Distinguish February, October, coastal/inland and actual uncertainty before using words such as “stronger”.
4. Use a short figure title with a concrete panel-by-panel caption. Include units, comparison population and the interval's meaning. Number figures, tables and square-bracket citations in order of appearance.
5. Put data restrictions next to the estimates they affect and close with specific limits on interpretation. Use “cities with more residents born in the destination province sent more visitors” or “an association between places”. Use stable data/code links in the declarations and reference list.
