## Frame and parental birthplace

The fixed frame contains 789,254 interprovincial pairs from 137 ECEPOV mainland cities to 5,849 mainland municipalities with at most 2,000 residents in 2021. It includes all eligible pairs regardless of publication. There are 87 months from July 2019 to September 2026. The sample contains 95,495 households in the origin cities. A strict parental tie appears in 7,988 pairs, of which 1,389 draw on at least two distinct households. A first-generation birthplace tie appears in 8,615 pairs, of which 711 draw on at least two households. [Frame and support](out/sample.csv).

I calculate the strict parental share among respondents born in their exact city of residence. Each parent slot contributes half the person's survey weight to its reported birthplace. The denominator is the sum of city-born respondents’ weights, including those with blank parent slots. A first-generation share uses the respondent’s own birthplace and the weights of all sampled city residents as its denominator. A survey tie requires a positive share. A sampled zero can arise because no such family lives in the city, the survey missed it or its birthplace was unavailable. The shares and household counts use exact INE codes and do not use the parents' province as a substitute for a missing municipality. [ECEPOV microdata](https://www.ine.es/ftp/microdatos/ecepov/datos_2021.zip) and [record layout](https://www.ine.es/ftp/microdatos/ecepov/disreg_ecepov21.zip), local copies checked 27 November 2026.

Among 110,742 sampled city-born respondents, 9.71% of parent slots are blank after weighting, and 86.83% of respondents have both slots reported. City-level blank-slot rates range from 2.98% to 28.30%. An additional 3.58% of weighted slots report a foreign birthplace. For all 235,877 respondents in the cities, the blank-slot share is 20.65%. City tables distinguish blank slots, foreign birthplaces, codes that match the 2021 municipal geography, and other codes. The blank rate describes data availability and includes blanks allowed by the survey design. [City-born completeness](out/parent_completeness_city_born.csv), [all-resident completeness](out/parent_completeness_all_residents.csv).

I used EPSG:3035 municipal polygon centroids and straight-line distances. The coast flag records whether the LAU polygon intersects a GISCO national coastal boundary tagged `COAS_FLAG=T` at the same scale and vintage. This yields 30 coastal villages and 49 coastal cities, with distances available for all pairs. The coast interaction uses the topology of the published 1:1 million layers, so small tidal channels or coastal features can differ from more detailed geography. The frame keeps 2021 municipal codes. A published row with a later code outside the 2021 code list is counted in the national audit and is not assigned to a 2021 municipality without a verified mapping. [Frame](out/sample.csv), [OD key audit](out/od_inventory.csv), [LAU polygons](https://gisco-services.ec.europa.eu/distribution/v2/lau/geojson/LAU_RG_01M_2021_3035.geojson), [coast boundary lines](https://gisco-services.ec.europa.eu/distribution/v2/nuts/geojson/NUTS_BN_01M_2021_3035_LEVL_0.geojson) and [GISCO terms](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/gisco/geodata/statistical-units), accessed 27 November 2026.

## What INE discloses

INE receives records grouped by operator. Section 5 of the technical project says that operators supply requested breakdowns where observations suffice and place smaller groups in residual categories. It gives no numeric operator threshold. Sections 6.2–6.4 say that INE expands province-pair totals using market-share corrections and allocates them to municipalities using the average of the operators’ relative shares, normalised within each province pair. Sections 7.3 and 7.5 describe the publication tables and state that crosses with fewer than 30 trips are withheld. The location of the rule in the publication section, together with the minimum released cell of exactly 30 in every month, supports reading 30 as a cutoff on the published expanded municipal cross, after its estimation. I found no statement that the earlier residual grouping uses the same cutoff, nor a guarantee that the omitted mass at that stage can all be represented as expanded municipal counts below 30. [INE technical project, §§5–7](https://www.ine.es/experimental/turismo_moviles/exp_moviles_turismo_interno.pdf), checked 27 November 2026. [Monthly released-cell audit](out/od_inventory.csv).

I sum released municipal trips on each available margin's own support. The national 52997 total has a gap of 22.72% to 42.44% across the 87 months. For the 137 origin cities, 53000 city totals have a gap of 10.78% to 37.27%. The 53001 all-origin totals for villages with a published margin have a gap of 38.41% to 61.08%. Province-to-village 53001 splits are available in the local extract for 12 months, with gaps of 32.78% to 45.83% on released margin cells. In August 2024 the national gap was 5,514,795 trips (24.65%), the origin-city total gap was 1,531,656 (12.00%), and the village arrival gap was 1,742,757 (41.37%). Some aligned municipal sums exceed their margins, including 104 village-total cells by more than ten trips. Larger discrepancies can reflect differences among releases, definitions of residuals, or vintages. The gap is a net coverage diagnostic. The published margins do not provide a total for the intersection of the 137 origins and the eligible villages, or allow one to recover particular unpublished cells. [Monthly margin audit](out/monthly_margins.csv), [52997](https://www.ine.es/jaxiT3/Tabla.htm?t=52997), [53000](https://www.ine.es/jaxiT3/Tabla.htm?t=53000), [53001](https://www.ine.es/jaxiT3/Tabla.htm?t=53001), catalogue checked on 27 November 2026.

On this strict frame, 17.16% of tied pairs were visible in both February and August 2024, compared with 0.69% of other pairs. In August alone, the corresponding shares were 31.12% and 2.14%. Of the 360 pairs visible in every month, 184 had a parental tie. [Visibility by month and tie status](out/visibility.csv), [sample](out/sample.csv).

## Estimation and inference

Within each dyad I subtract the same-year reference outcome from the target month. The dyad effect cancels. In each contrast I absorb city × destination-province and village fixed effects, equivalent to interacting those effects with month in a paired panel with dyad effects. The regressors include the parental-tie indicator, a first-generation tie indicator and `log(1 + 10,000 × first-generation share)`, log distance, its square and its interaction with coast. The latter allows a coastal distance gradient to vary across months. A separate coast level is absorbed by the village effects. Pooled comparisons weight years equally on the same frame. The count analysis models trip changes in levels. I use log counts for the selected continuously published comparison. [Estimation code](run.py), [fixed effects and inference](model.py).

For the interval count regression, each published value is fixed at its release value, and each unpublished value ranges from 0 to 29 under the working censoring assumption. The August-minus-reference outcome interval for each dyad is therefore bounded by its completed August minimum minus completed reference maximum, and vice versa. The partial regression coefficient is a linear combination of those changes. For its exact lower bound I assign the low endpoint where the parental coefficient weight is positive and the high endpoint where it is negative. I reverse this assignment for the upper bound. These bounds are sharp for the stated per-cell intervals without cross-cell restrictions. Constant fills of 0 and 29 are two scenarios within the interval framework. They do not bound a partial regression coefficient because some regression weights are negative and because the baseline also contains unpublished cells. I report endpoint intervals separately. The lower endpoint's lower confidence limit and upper endpoint's upper confidence limit give a conservative pointwise envelope under the maintained censoring and inference assumptions. [Implementation](model.py), [estimates](out/estimates.csv).

The intervals use the origin-province plus destination-province minus province-pair covariance estimate and finite-sample corrections. I add the variance from 399 within-city household resamples with replacement, rebuilding both shares, their denominators and reporting-household counts, and refitting each model. The same draw applies to every month. The city is the sampling stratum for the covered municipalities and capitals, where INE selected primary dwellings with systematic random starts. I preserve the supplied final weights rather than recalibrating each draw. The resulting t intervals are a pointwise approximation for this two-source analysis. Households absent from the released sample cannot enter the bootstrap, and uncertainty about missing parent reports, raw operator counts and the latent true tie is outside it. [ECEPOV design and weighting, §6](https://www.ine.es/metodologia/metodologia_ECEPOV_2021.pdf), accessed 27 November 2026. [Variance components and refit counts](out/estimates.csv).

## Seasonality, dose and matched villages

Restricting to 2022 onward gives publication changes of +3.75 pp [2.17, 5.33] against February and +3.04 pp [1.60, 4.49] against October. The 2020–2021 comparisons give +4.56 pp [2.37, 6.75] and +2.95 pp [1.10, 4.80]. The monthly series includes publication and both constant count fills, with pointwise intervals and both same-year baselines wherever observed. [Pooled estimates](out/estimates.csv), [monthly series](out/calendar.csv).

Conditional on the presence of a tie, one unit of `log(1 + 10,000 × parental share)` is associated with an extra +2.07 pp [0.58, 3.57] against February and +2.51 pp [1.43, 3.58] against October. A tie recorded by at least two households is associated with an additional +4.52 pp [0.35, 8.69] and +3.44 pp [−0.51, 7.40], respectively, compared with a tie recorded by one household. The continuous share gives negative count point estimates under both fills with intervals spanning zero. Both markers reflect the intensity and detectability of a tie in the survey. [Dose estimates](out/dose.csv).

I paired 4,617 of the 7,988 parental ties to a village without a reported tie from the same city, within the same destination province, coastal class and first-generation tie status. Matching without replacement uses calipers of 0.25 in log population plus one, 0.10 in log distance and 10 pp in non-primary housing share. It uses no trips. The placebo term labels the matched untied village. The regression includes separate terms for matched reported ties, matched placebo villages and unmatched reported ties. The original matching is fixed for the bootstrap and the exposures are rebuilt. The placebo also predicts release, by +2.19 pp [1.63, 2.75] against February and +1.90 pp [1.50, 2.30] against October. The reported-tie slope exceeds the placebo slope by +1.82 pp [0.73, 2.90] and +1.47 pp [0.23, 2.70]. Some placebo villages can have unobserved ties, and matching on the recorded attributes can leave other common seasonal factors. [Matched balance](out/match_balance.csv), [falsification estimates](out/falsification.csv), [Census housing table 59525](https://www.ine.es/jaxi/Tabla.htm?tpx=59525), local copy checked 27 November 2026.

The evidence is about the association of a survey-measured birthplace link with aggregate data release and, under specified completions, with aggregate trips. It cannot establish who travelled, whether a traveller visited a parent’s village, what prompted a trip or where a traveller stayed.

## Choices relative to the frozen plan

- I used paired differences, the alternative in the design brief. The gate uses every available same-year August pair. February starts in 2020 because the series begins in July 2019. October ends in 2025 because the series ends in September 2026. The files report 2020–2021 separately and include a 2022 onward sensitivity. The monthly series uses both references wherever available and flags 2020–2021.
- The primary gate tests presence of a sampled parental tie, as condition 1 states. I used the weighted first-generation share and its indicator jointly. I tested the weighted parental share and multiple reporting households as separate dose checks conditional on tie presence.
- I used an interval-response linear projection with sharp coefficient bounds because a parametric extrapolation below the release threshold would be sensitive to assumptions about the missing part of the distribution. I treat the 0 and 29 fills as two scenarios of one constant-fill method and require the positive sign to survive interval regression and constant fills for both baselines. The preregistration leaves the choice of treatment open. This interpretation asks whether the sign is robust to heterogeneous suppressed counts, in addition to the two uniform replacements.
- The audit uses frozen local extracts. The province-by-village breakdown of 53001 is incomplete in that extract. The national-origin village totals provide a comparison in all months. I did not derive counts for unpublished dyads from the higher-level tables.
- The coastline test uses GISCO LAU and GISCO coastal boundary lines. Same-code joins hold the 2021 frame fixed, with no geographic imputation for unmatched codes. The housing measure is the non-primary dwelling share, which includes vacant dwellings.
- The selected-panel interval uses identified resamples when at least 95% of draws identify the parental coefficient. The CSV gives the number of identified draws and the intervals conditional on the original survey wherever these are needed for comparison.
