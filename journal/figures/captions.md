# Figure captions

## Figure 1. Linking overnight stays to birthplaces

**a** Mainland provinces, shaded by net overnight stays per resident on 14–15 August 2024. Arrows connect cities to destination provinces. Their widths scale with the excess persons per night in the August window (nine nights ending on 10–18 August) over 92 ordinary nights in 2024, for the 12 largest excesses among 137 origin cities. **b** The model relates a city's overnight flow to the share of its residents born in the destination province, comparing cities within the same origin province and controlling for distance and income. **c** MITMA daily files and INE monthly files available from 2022 through 2025. Hatching marks the MITMA method introduced on 1 July 2025. Daily dates denote the morning at the end of the night.

## Figure 2. The roots calendar

**a** Nightly roots elasticity for 112 cities in 22 origin provinces, with 95% origin-province-clustered confidence ribbons. Open points show the 2025 series, including nights outside the hold-out contrast. Dashed lines show each year's ordinary-night mean. Holiday symbols mark nights ending on Good Friday, All Saints' Day or Christmas morning. **b** August minus ordinary-night contrasts and overlapping nine-night ordinary placebo windows. The 2022–2024 tests use new ordinary nights and previously seen August nights. The 2025 hold-out uses five unseen August nights and 62 unseen ordinary nights. Each placebo window has nine eligible ordinary nights. Intervals are 95% cluster-robust intervals for the separate 2022–2024 years and 95% wild-cluster bootstrap intervals for pooled 2022–2024 and 2025. **c** Joint arrival-cohort terms for 675 cities. Lines show centred seven-night rolling medians within continuous coverage, with gaps at the method change. Grey bands mark August test dates and Christmas Eve through Epiphany eve. Open markers mark 2025.

## Figure 3. Which birthplaces gain weight in August

All horizontal axes show changes in roots elasticity. **a** Arrival-cohort and birthplace-size contrasts for 675 cities across 44 origin provinces. Filled points show 2022–2024 confirmation against new ordinary nights. Open points show the 2025 hold-out. The urban-born contrast is the companion term of the village-born model. **b** Contrasts for 14–15 August 2024 against 13–14 February and the mean of 12 October weekday nights. Inland and coastal labels refer to destination zones. The direct difference uses the 19 provinces with both types of destination. **c** Pre-1981 minus later-arrival contrasts for the same nights within two age bands. The later group is 2016–2023 arrivals for ages 43–64 and arrivals from 1981 onwards for ages 65 and over. All intervals use origin-province clusters. The urban-born row shows 95% cluster-robust intervals. The other rows use 95% wild-cluster bootstrap intervals.

## Figure 4. The receiving end of holiday mobility

**a** Zones in the most emptied fifth (475 of 2,376 zones ranked by population loss from their 1900–1960 peak), coloured by net overnight balance on three nights in 2024. **b** Share receiving more people than they send on each of 1,079 valid nights in 2022–2024, for all 475 zones and for the 84 comprising a single municipality. The bracket spans the four ordinary baselines in Analysis D, including the October weekday mean. **c** Mean arrivals and departures per resident in the most emptied fifth, with 95% within-province zone-bootstrap intervals. The table below the panel gives adjusted changes per unit of population-loss rank in 2,053 inland zones.

## Figure S1. Network structure on ordinary and holiday nights

**a, b** Incoming and outgoing degree (plus one, on logarithmic axes) in 2,435 mainland zones on 13–14 February and 14–15 August 2024. Degree uses directed links carrying at least ten overnight travellers. Points are coloured by zone type: inland, coastal, or a city of more than 100,000 residents. **c** Undirected degree assortativity on those nights and All Saints (dots), compared with the mean of 50 connected degree-preserving rewirings per night (ticks). Grey bars span the range reachable by degree-preserving rewiring, from maximally disassortative to maximally assortative. **d** Mean second-order degree mixing (dots), compared with the mean of 50 connected rewirings preserving degree and the rich-club curve (ticks).

## Figure S2. Sensitivity of the August roots contrasts

**a, b** Pooled August premium (P1, main sample of 112 cities) and pre-1981 arrival premium (P2, main sample of 675 cities) in the 2022–2024 confirmation sample. Diamonds mark the main specifications. Intervals are 95% wild-cluster bootstrap intervals by origin province. Each lower strip shows point estimates from 47 fits, each excluding one destination province. The dark point repeats the full-sample estimate.

## Figure S3. Sensitivity to the treatment of zero census cells

**a–d** Change from the baseline to 14–15 August 2024 in the predicted-flow contrast for a one-standard-deviation rise in the raw birth share, plotted as a percentage. The raw-share shift is flow-weighted and defined within fixed effects. Points compare 675 cities, or a restricted 112-city sample on the final row, against 13–14 February or the mean of 12 October weekday nights. Lines are 95% wild-cluster bootstrap intervals by origin province, transformed to the common predicted-flow scale.

## Figure S4. Parental ties and published city-to-village trips

**a, b** Percentage-point change in the association between a survey-reported parental tie and publication of an INE flow, relative to February or October of the same year, for 789,254 city–village pairs. Points mark August. Ribbons show 95% intervals combining origin- and destination-province clustering with variance from 399 household resamples. Gaps indicate unavailable comparison months or the reference month itself. **c** August minus reference trip-count bounds under suppression. Dots mark estimated endpoints. The light segment spans the lower endpoint's 95% lower limit to the upper endpoint's 95% upper limit.

## Figure S5. MITMA method bridge

**a, b** Pooled elasticity (112 cities) and the pre-1981 term (675 cities) on six earlier nights processed by both MITMA methods. Each line joins two estimates for the same night. The panels use different horizontal ranges.
