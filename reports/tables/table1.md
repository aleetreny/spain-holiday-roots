# Table 1. Basic properties of the overnight-stay networks

| | Ordinary weekday<br>13–14 Feb | Summer holiday<br>14–15 Aug | All Saints<br>31 Oct–1 Nov |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nodes N in the giant component | 2,432 | 2,435 | 2,434 |
| Links L | 26,465 | 101,996 | 48,698 |
| Density (%) | 0.89 | 3.44 | 1.64 |
| Average degree ⟨k⟩ | 21.7 | 83.8 | 40.0 |
| Maximum degree k_max / share of all zones | 1,499 / 62 % | 2,233 / 92 % | 2,008 / 82 % |
| Median out- / in-degree | 7 / 7 | 14 / 27 | 10 / 16 |
| Reciprocity (rewired) | 0.52 (0.13) | 0.24 (0.09) | 0.48 (0.15) |

| Rich-club clique n_clique | 17 | 17† | 22 |
| Average path length ℓ | 2.44 | 2.00 | 2.16 |
| Expected in a random graph ℓ* | 2.53 | 1.76 | 2.11 |

One network per night of 2024. Node: one of 2,435 zones of mainland Spain; link i→j: at least 10 residents of i sleep in j. Undirected projection of all 2,435 zones (links, density) except the median out- / in-degree and reciprocity; N, ℓ and ℓ* refer to its giant component. In brackets: mean of 50 random networks in which every zone keeps its number of links (degree-preserving rewiring); for clustering, also 50 Erdős–Rényi networks. ℓ* = ln N / ln⟨k⟩ approximates the path length of a random graph (Zhou & Mondragón 2007). Rich-club clique: the largest n such that the n best-connected zones are all linked to one another. † The change from the ordinary night does not keep its direction on every pair of similar nights.
